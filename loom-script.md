# Loom Script — "Let agents bank, not just pay"
**Target: 5:00 max. Spoken ~720 words ≈ 4:40 at a calm pace, leaving headroom.**
Format: talk to camera, no slides (brief says none required). Conviction > polish.

---

## Production notes (read once before recording)
- **The first 30 seconds are explicitly graded.** Open on a person, not a thesis. Do not say "Hi, in this video I'll…". Just start with Sofía.
- **Energy:** lean in, talk like you're convincing a smart friend this *has* to exist — not presenting a report.
- **Banned phrases:** "as you can see in the memo," "synergy," "leverage," "at the end of the day," any hedging ("I think maybe," "sort of").
- **Pace:** slow down on the two emotional beats (the $12K button-click; the closing line). Speed up through the rejected-alternatives.
- **If you run long, cut:** the two sentences in [2:30 Why this one] down to one. Protect the hook and the close.
- **Optional on-screen:** when you say the numbers (~$60K, $1.35B), you *can* hold up a sticky note or cut to the impact-model — but it's optional; the story carries it.

## THE SCRIPT

### [0:00–0:20] — COLD OPEN (the hook)
> Right now, in São Paulo, a finance lead named Sofía is clicking "approve" on a payment her AI already checked.
> She'll do it six hours a week. Every week.
> Her company pays her about **twelve thousand dollars a year**… to click a button.
>
> Here's the absurd part: her agent is smart enough to *run* that payment. It's just not *allowed to have money.*

*(Pause half a beat after "have money." Let it land.)*

### [0:20–1:00] — THE TURN (the opportunity)
> We taught AI agents to think. We taught them to work. This year we even taught them to *pay* — Visa, Mastercard, Google, Coinbase all shipped agent-payment rails.
> But every one of them solved the same half of the problem: how an agent *spends* — on a human's card, with a human clicking "approve."
>
> Nobody solved the other half. **An agent has no account of its own.** No identity a supplier can trust. No balance that earns. No way to move real money into the real economy.
> We taught agents to pay. We never let them **bank.**

### [1:00–2:10] — THE FEATURE
> So here's the one feature I'd build at Kira: **give every AI agent its own real bank account.**
> Three things, that today don't exist together anywhere:
> One — a **verified identity**, issued under the business that owns the agent, so a counterparty actually trusts it.
> Two — a **balance that earns** — idle money sits at around seven percent, so the agent is a little treasury, not a pass-through wallet.
> Three — an **off-ramp into local money** — it can pay out in seconds to PIX in Brazil, SPEI in Mexico, cash across thirty-five-plus countries.
>
> Picture it: Sofía's agent pays a supplier in São Paulo, holds the float overnight at seven percent, and settles the change to a wallet in Bogotá — and nobody clicked anything.
> The first time you watch that happen, you can't un-see how broken the old way was.

### [2:10–2:40] — WHY ONLY KIRA (+ rejected)
> Why Kira, and not a neobank or a card network? Because this needs four things stacked that only Kira has together: **KYB, a yield wallet, local rails in thirty-five countries, and an agent-native core.**
> A neobank isn't agent-native. A card network can't settle locally in LatAm. A crypto wallet has no compliance and no off-ramp.
> I looked hard at the obvious alternatives — a treasury copilot, self-reconciling payouts — and killed them. The copilot, Kira basically already sells. The rest had no story you could *feel.* This one only Kira can ship.

### [2:40–3:40] — IMPACT (floor, then ceiling)
> Now the math — and I'm giving you the number that needs *zero* assumptions first.
> For any SMB that turns this on, it cuts cross-border B2B cost from about **five percent to half a percent.** That's roughly **sixty thousand dollars a year** back to a mid-sized business — and even if I'm half wrong on every input, it's still thirty grand.
> And here's what that *unlocks*: a sub-five-hundred-dollar supplier payment goes from costing twenty-five dollars to costing one. For the first time, the micro-importer — the business too small to trade internationally today — can.
>
> That's the floor. The ceiling: at just **one percent** of LatAm's twenty-seven million small businesses, this is a **one-point-three-five-billion-dollar** recurring line. Even a tenth of that is forty-five times Kira's revenue today.

### [3:40–4:25] — WHAT WOULD HAVE TO BE TRUE (kill the objections)
> Two questions you're already asking.
> "Is this legal — a robot with a bank account?" It's not a robot account. It's a **corporate account with programmable delegation** — banks already do delegated authority. No new legal category. Start with one lighthouse customer, prove it, build the precedent.
> "Won't OpenAI just do this?" OpenAI builds *intelligence*, not LatAm rails. Their agent wallet will be generic dollars. It won't have PIX, SPEI, or a banking license in Peru. The likely ending isn't them beating Kira — it's them *partnering* with Kira as the execution layer.

### [4:25–5:00] — CLOSE (make them want it)
> The agents are already here. Sofía's company is running them today — on borrowed human logins, because there's no alternative.
> Kira's bet isn't inventing a future. It's *formalizing a present* everyone's pretending isn't happening.
> So here's my ten-year claim: a billion people won't log into a bank. **Their agents will.** And the agents working for Latin America should bank at Kira.
> Let's stop making Sofía click the button.

*(Stop. Don't add "thanks for watching." End on "the button.")*
