# AI Debate Log 4 — A2A: the hardest teardown, and the defense
The decisive exchange. The model was asked to attack A2A with its four strongest
arguments; the candidate had to defend each or kill the idea. A2A survived this — that's
why it's the pick.

## THE TEARDOWN (model argues to kill A2A)

### Argument 1 — The market does not exist yet
Infrastructure for a market with no customers today. How many AI agents are autonomously
transacting in LatAm right now? Zero with their own accounts. Kira is seed-stage ($8.7M
funding, $3M ARR). Betting the roadmap on a market that may exist in 3–5 years is a luxury
startups don't have. The evaluator asks "Who pays for this next quarter?" — and there's no answer.

### Argument 2 — LatAm regulators have no framework for non-human accounts
KYC in Colombia, Mexico, Peru requires a verifiable human. The SBS, CNBV, and Colombia's
Superintendencia Financiera have no legal category for an "AI agent" as account holder.
A2A in LatAm isn't a product problem — it's a multi-year regulatory problem. Meanwhile
Bridge and Coinbase move in markets with clearer regulation.

### Argument 3 — Kira's current customers are not ready
Banco Industrial, Vank, Factil — do they have autonomous agents transacting today? No.
Operations are mostly manual/semi-automated. This is a feature Kira's best customers can't
use in the next 18 months. A feature with no adoption isn't bold — it's premature.

### Argument 4 — OpenAI / Anthropic will run you over
Model providers have massive distribution. When they decide their agents need wallets,
they'll embed "agent wallets" directly — or acquire someone who built them. Kira, at $8.7M,
can't win that race if the giants run. The first-mover window could close with one OpenAI announcement.

## THE DEFENSE (candidate rebuts each)

### Defense 1 — vs "market doesn't exist"
The market doesn't exist with the right infrastructure yet — but the agents already exist.
Kira's customers already run treasury, procurement, and compliance agents — they're just
using human credentials because there's no alternative. Kira isn't betting on a future
market; it's formalizing what's already happening informally. AWS didn't wait for companies
to "need cloud" before building it.

### Defense 2 — vs "regulators"
An A2A account isn't a new legal entity — it's a corporate account with programmable
delegation rules. Banks already run multi-user accounts with delegated authorization; Kira
extends that existing framework. Start with one lighthouse customer under its own regulatory
umbrella, prove the model, build the precedent. Regulatory complexity is the moat, not the blocker.

### Defense 3 — vs "customers not ready"
Factil, Vank, Borderless are exactly LatAm's most tech-forward companies. You won't get
every customer on day one. One lighthouse — "my payroll agent runs payroll across 8 countries
with no manual approval" — creates the case study that sells the next customer. The pain is
real today: CFOs manually approve transactions their systems already validated.

### Defense 4 — vs "OpenAI runs you over"
OpenAI builds intelligence, not financial rails. Its "agent wallet" will be generic USD
infrastructure. LatAm needs PIX in Brazil, SPEI in Mexico, SBS compliance in Peru, local
banking relationships. Kira has that; OpenAI won't build it in 18 months. The likely outcome
isn't competition — it's partnership: Kira becomes the financial execution layer for OpenAI
agents in LatAm.

## RESOLUTION — what survives into the memo
- "Who pays next quarter?" → the Year-1 beachhead: existing agents using human credentials
  today, formalized into agent accounts.
- "Regulatory wall" → reframed: corporate account + programmable delegation, lighthouse-first.
- "Premature" → reframed: pain is real today (manual approval of pre-validated transactions).
- "Giants run you over" → reframed: rails ≠ intelligence; partnership > competition.
These four defenses ARE the memo's "What would have to be true" section.
